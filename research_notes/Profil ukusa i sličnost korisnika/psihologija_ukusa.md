# Psychology and sociology of taste: dimensions, reliability, personality links, cross-domain prediction


## 1. The MUSIC model and STOMP: music preference structure

### Takeaway
Music preferences have a robust latent structure of about five factors (MUSIC: Mellow, Unpretentious, Sophisticated, Intense, Contemporary). It replicates across samples and at very large scale. Its genre-free successor (Arousal / Valence / Depth, built from rated musical attributes) is the better basis for a cross-artist taste space, because genre labels are noisy.

### Cited Findings
- **MUSIC model.** Rentfrow, Goldberg & Levitin (2011), "The Structure of Musical Preferences: A Five-Factor Model," JPSP 100(6), 1139–1157, doi 10.1037/a0022406.
  - The model rests on listeners' affective reactions to short music excerpts across many genres, not on genre labels. Across three studies a five-factor latent structure emerged that is "not tied to genre" and mainly reflects affective responses.
  - A fourth study showed that preferences for each factor are shaped by both social and auditory characteristics of the music. — [FORRT replication atlas entry](https://forrt.org/flora-replication-atlas/doi/10.1037/a0022406/); [APA Monitor 2011](https://www.apa.org/monitor/2011/05/music)
- **What the factors mean.** MUSIC stands for Mellow, Unpretentious, Sophisticated, Intense, Contemporary. Typical exemplar genres:
  - Mellow: soft rock, R&B, adult contemporary
  - Unpretentious: country, folk
  - Sophisticated: classical, jazz, opera
  - Intense: rock, punk, metal
  - Contemporary: rap, electronic, funk

  These exemplars are illustrative only, because the factors cut across genres. The APA Monitor gives the example that one country artist can load Mellow+Unpretentious while another loads Sophisticated+Unpretentious. — [Frontiers in Psychology 2018 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01488/full); [APA Monitor](https://www.apa.org/monitor/2011/05/music)
- **Replication.** The five-factor structure was replicated by Kosinski and colleagues in Music Perception ("The Song Remains the Same: A Replication and Extension of the MUSIC Model"), which also identified the musical attributes that distinguish each factor. — [Stanford GSB listing](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/index%2Ephp/node/428616)
- **Cross-cultural replication.** Greenberg et al. (2022) is the most important large-scale test: "Universals and variations in musical preferences: A study of preferential reactions to Western music in 53 countries," JPSP, doi 10.1037/pspp0000397.
  - Sample: more than 350,000 participants rated 23 genres and completed the TIPI (a 10-item Big Five measure).
  - Personality–preference links were largely consistent across world regions.
  - Extraversion → Contemporary.
  - Agreeableness → Mellow and Unpretentious.
  - Openness → Mellow, Contemporary, Intense and Sophisticated.
  - Conscientiousness → negatively related to Intense.
  - Neuroticism results were mixed.
  - The Extraversion–Contemporary link was stronger near the equator.
  - Caveat: the TIPI is a very short measure, which attenuates correlations. — [ScienceDaily (Cambridge release)](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2022/02/220209093441.htm); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/942746); [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2022-02-musical-personalities-worldwide.pdf)
- **Attribute-based follow-up.** Greenberg et al. (2016), "The Song Is You: Preferences for Musical Attribute Dimensions Reflect Personality," Social Psychological and Personality Science, doi 10.1177/1948550616641473.
  - More than 100 excerpts from 20+ genres and subgenres were rated on 38 attributes. These grouped into three dimensions:
    - Arousal: energy and intensity
    - Valence: positive vs. negative emotion
    - Depth: sophistication and emotional depth
  - Roughly 10,000 Facebook-recruited participants each rated 50 excerpts.
  - Openness → preference for Depth.
  - Extraverted excitement-seekers → preference for high Arousal.
  - Neuroticism → positively related to Arousal and negatively to Valence (per Psychology Today summary). — [Cambridge news](https://cam.ac.uk/node/177582); [Psychology Today "Are musical genres outdated?"](https://psychologytoday.com/za/blog/your-musical-self/201605/are-musical-genres-outdated)
  - One secondary summary describes the structure as two components, with Depth as a sub-part of Valence. The Cambridge release describes three dimensions. Treat the exact structure as "2–3 dimensions, robustly Arousal + Valence, with Depth as a third." — same sources
- **Predecessor model.** Rentfrow & Gosling (2003, JPSP), STOMP (Short Test Of Music Preferences), proposed four genre-based dimensions:
  - Reflective & Complex
  - Intense & Rebellious
  - Upbeat & Conventional
  - Energetic & Rhythmic

  MUSIC (2011) superseded it. — [Wikipedia: Psychology of music preference (secondary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology_of_music_preference)

### Inferences
- For a product, the most defensible music axes are:
  - (a) Arousal/energy
  - (b) Valence/mood
  - (c) Depth/sophistication
  - optionally (d) MUSIC-style "style families" (Mellow / Unpretentious / Sophisticated / Intense / Contemporary)
- Arousal and valence map directly onto audio features available from streaming APIs (energy, valence, acousticness, etc.), which lets playlist data be projected into a validated space without genre tags.
- Genre-label taxonomies are the weakest representation: MUSIC was explicitly derived to escape them.

### Gaps
- Exact MUSIC loadings, variance explained, and congruence coefficients for the 2011 paper and the Kosinski replication were not retrieved.
- Exact effect sizes in Greenberg 2016 and 2022 were not retrieved; press coverage gives only directions. Expect correlations of roughly .05–.20 given TIPI and the meta-analysis below (inference, not verified).
- Non-Western music was not tested in Greenberg 2022: the stimuli were Western genres.

## 2. Cross-domain entertainment factors (Rentfrow, Goldberg & Zilca 2011)

### Takeaway
Across music, film, TV and books, liking organizes into five content-based ("substance over style") dimensions: Communal, Aesthetic, Dark, Thrilling, Cerebral. The structure is highly stable across samples. Personality explains substantial variance for four of the five; Thrilling is the weakest and least replicable. Video games were not included.

### Cited Findings
- **Citation and samples.** Journal of Personality 79(2), 2011, doi 10.1111/j.1467-6494.2010.00662.x. Three samples:
  - University: N = 1,946, mean age 19
  - Community: N = 736, mean age 60
  - Internet: N = 545, mean age 34
  - Combined N = 3,227
- **Measure and analysis.** Participants rated 108 genres on 7-point scales: 22 music, 34 books/magazines, 18 film, 34 TV. **No games.** The analysis was PCA with varimax (orthogonal) rotation, retaining 5 factors. — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2964424)
- **The five factors**, with example genres (same source):
  - **Communal**: people and relationship content. Daytime talk and made-for-TV movies, romance and family films, pop and show tunes, romance and cooking books.
  - **Aesthetic**: creative, abstract, demanding. Classical, opera and blues; art and poetry books; foreign and classic films; arts/humanities TV.
  - **Dark**: edgy, intense. Punk and heavy metal; horror and erotica (books and film); late-night talk.
  - **Thrilling**: action, spy, sci-fi (film, TV, books); thriller and espionage books. No music genre loads primarily here.
  - **Cerebral**: information-oriented. Business/economy, news/current events, health (TV and books); documentaries.
- **Stability.** Mean cross-sample factor congruence for the 5-factor solution was .95. Residualizing age and sex left the structure nearly unchanged (mean congruence .90). Only 9 of 108 genres (8%) cross-loaded ≥ .40 on more than one factor. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2964424)
- **Weak point.** The personality-correlate profile of Thrilling did not generalize across samples (column-vector r = −.03, vs. an average of .62 for the other four). — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2964424)
- **Personality facet correlates** (IPIP-AB5C; Internet N ≈ 503, community N ≈ 449):

  | Factor | Facet | r |
  |---|---|---|
  | Communal | Tenderness | .49 (both samples) |
  | Communal | Warmth | .34–.36 |
  | Communal | Imperturbability | −.31 to −.33 |
  | Aesthetic | Reflection | .36 / .54 |
  | Aesthetic | Creativity | .36 |
  | Dark | Dutifulness | −.20 |
  | Dark | Cooperation | −.23 / −.28 |
  | Cerebral | Intellect | .15–.19 |

  Openness–Aesthetic was r = .50 with self-report and r = .46 with peer-report (N = 598), so the effect is not only shared self-report method variance. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2964424)
- **Variance explained by personality.** Multiple R from personality, by factor:

  | Factor | Multiple R |
  |---|---|
  | Communal | .55–.64 (R² up to ~.41) |
  | Aesthetic | .49–.60 |
  | Dark | .50–.51 |
  | Thrilling | .27–.49 |
  | Cerebral | .30–.33 |

  Personality added significant variance over demographics for every factor except Thrilling in the Internet sample. Caveat: these are in-sample Rs from facet-level predictors, so some overfitting inflation is likely. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2964424)
- **Demographic correlates.** Mean |r| by demographic: gender .27, intelligence .16, education .15, age .13, ethnicity .04.
  - Women score higher on Communal (r = .41–.59) and Aesthetic.
  - Men score higher on Dark and Thrilling.
  - Education is related to Communal (r = −.13 to −.29) and Aesthetic (r = +.20 to +.30).
  - Younger age → Dark (r ≈ −.38).
  - Older age → Cerebral (r = .11–.16). — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2964424)
- **Authors' conclusion.** Preferences are "more a function of substance than style": people seek a type of content regardless of medium. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2964424); [BPS Research Digest](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/dark-cerebral-what-kind-media-consumer-are-you)
- **Movie preferences at scale.** A later cross-cultural study (9,201 participants, 23 countries, linked to Big Five data from about 3.5M Facebook users) found personality–movie-preference links to be modest. This is secondary coverage only; the primary paper was not retrieved. — [Stewart-Williams summary](https://www.stevestewartwilliams.com/p/what-do-your-favorite-movies-say-about-you)

### Inferences
- This is the strongest empirical template for a cross-domain taste vector. A 4–5-dimensional content space (Communal, Aesthetic, Dark, Cerebral, plus a less reliable Thrilling) can be scored from genre/tag preferences in any medium.
- Because the factors are orthogonal by construction and partly demographic (gender, age, education), a similarity metric should consider residualizing on age and gender. Otherwise "similarity" partly reflects being the same age and sex.
- The 2011 sample was US-only, used genre ratings rather than specific titles, and pre-dates streaming. Mapping specific titles and artists to factors requires a genre/tag → factor loading table.

### Gaps
- No published replication of the 5 cross-domain factors with games, podcasts or live events was found.
- Test–retest reliability of the factor scores was not reported in the paper.
- I found no study directly testing music-only → film/book prediction accuracy (for example, out-of-sample r).

## 3. Big Five links: effect sizes, meta-analysis and critiques

### Takeaway
Personality–taste correlations at the genre or factor level are mostly small (|r| < .10–.20). Openness ↔ sophisticated/aesthetic/complex taste is the one robust, moderate link (r ≈ .3–.5 at the factor level with good measures). A taste profile carries real but limited personality signal.

### Cited Findings
- **Meta-analysis.** Schäfer & Mehlhorn (2017), "Can personality traits predict musical style preferences? A meta-analysis," Personality and Individual Differences 116, 265–273. — [record](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_journals_2051182577); [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology_of_music_preference)
  - It pooled studies relating the Big Five and sensation seeking to the MUSIC dimensions, across 30 sub-analyses.
  - Only 6 of 30 pooled coefficients exceeded |.10|.
  - The authors concluded that personality barely accounts for music preference, and proposed musical *functions* (why people use music) as a better explanation.
- **Critiques of the meta-analysis.** Later work noted that the included studies relied mostly on self-report and simple statistics, and mostly on adult samples. A 2020 Spotify-behaviour study argued that personality still matters when measured on streaming behaviour. — [Wikipedia summary of critiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology_of_music_preference); [Anderson et al. 2020 PDF (Spotify)](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/personality/conscientiousness/2020-anderson.pdf)
- **Contrast with Rentfrow et al. 2011.** Correlations are much larger at the facet level and with aggregate cross-media factors: Openness–Aesthetic r ≈ .50; Tenderness–Communal r ≈ .49. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2964424)
- **Small item-level correlations.** In Nave et al. (2018), trait-specific correlations with liking of individual musical excerpts rarely exceeded r ≈ .06. Openness and extraversion were associated with general liking of music. — [Nave et al. 2018 PDF](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/personality/conscientiousness/2018-nave.pdf)

### Inferences
- Single-genre correlations are tiny, but aggregated factors and many-item ML predictions reach r ≈ .25–.45 (see section 4).
- Personality should not be shown to users as a confident read-out from taste. It is better framed as a soft, probabilistic layer, with openness the most trustworthy.

### Gaps
- No meta-analysis of film, book or game preferences × Big Five was found.

## 4. Digital-footprint prediction accuracy (Kosinski 2013, Youyou 2015, Nave 2018)

### Takeaway
Many-item behavioural taste data predict Big Five traits out of sample at r ≈ .2–.45. Openness is always the best-predicted trait. Music-only likes reach about r = .30 for openness, roughly a coworker's accuracy. Accuracy rises with the number of likes.

### Cited Findings
- **Kosinski, Stillwell & Graepel (2013), PNAS 110(15), 5802–5805.** N = 58,466 US Facebook users, median 68 likes each, modelled with SVD (100 components) plus linear or logistic regression. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3625324)
  - Openness r = .43, versus test–retest reliability of .50.
  - Extraversion r = .40.
  - Other traits and life satisfaction r = .17–.30.
  - Intelligence r = .39.
  - Age r = .75.
  - Classification accuracy: gender 93%, ethnicity 95%, Democrat vs. Republican 85%, sexual orientation 88% (men) / 75% (women).
- **Youyou, Kosinski & Stillwell (2015), PNAS.** — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4313801); [Stanford Daily](https://stanforddaily.com/2015/01/14/researchers-find-computers-are-better-predictors-of-personality-than-friends-family/)
  - Like-based computer judgments correlated with self-ratings at r = .56 (averaged across traits), versus r = .49 for Facebook friends.
  - The computer surpassed a spouse at about 300 likes; it surpassed colleagues, friends and family with fewer.
  - Reported N varies across sources (70,520 with computer judgments; abstract 86,220).
- **Nave et al. (2018), "Musical Preferences Predict Personality: Evidence From Active Listening and Facebook Likes," Psychological Science 29(7), 1145–1158.** — [Nave et al. 2018 PDF](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/personality/conscientiousness/2018-nave.pdf)
  - **Study 1:** N ≈ 22,252, reactions to unfamiliar excerpts, out-of-sample accuracy:

    | Trait | r |
    |---|---|
    | Openness | .25 [.23, .26] |
    | Extraversion | .16 |
    | Agreeableness | .15 |
    | Neuroticism | .12 |
    | Conscientiousness | .11 |

    The gain from specific (versus general) liking was largest for openness (Δr = .09) and extraversion (Δr = .08), and only Δr = .01–.03 for the rest. Music adds significantly over age and gender for all traits.
  - **Study 2:** N = 21,929, Facebook likes of musical artists (users with ≥ 20 music likes):

    | Trait | r |
    |---|---|
    | Openness | .30 [.29, .31] |
    | Extraversion | .21 |
    | Conscientiousness | .19 |
    | Neuroticism | .18 |
    | Agreeableness | .17 |

    Openness and neuroticism accuracy was about a coworker's level; other traits reached 55–77% of a coworker's. For neuroticism, likes added nothing beyond age and gender.

### Inferences
- With a few hundred heterogeneous items (artists, films, books), expect trait inference around r ≈ .3–.45 for openness and ≈ .2–.3 for other traits. That is useful for matching but has large individual error.
- Music-only data are weaker than all-domain likes (.30 vs .43 for openness), which supports combining domains.
- Ethics and privacy: the same data reveal sexual orientation, politics and religion (Kosinski 2013), so this needs careful handling under GDPR.

### Gaps
- No equivalent large-sample accuracy numbers were found for film-only or book-only data.

## 5. Sociology: Bourdieu, omnivores, homophily and shared taste → ties

### Takeaway
Sociology finds that taste clusters across domains along class (cultural capital), age and gender axes. The elite pattern is now better described as breadth ("omnivorousness") than exclusive highbrow snobbery, though the concept is contested. Shared taste predicts tie formation modestly and domain-specifically: music and film yes, books no. Shared music taste bonds people mainly by signalling shared values.

### Cited Findings
- **Bourdieu in the UK and Australia.** *Culture, Class, Distinction* (Bennett, Savage, Silva, Warde, Gayo-Cal, Wright 2009) used multiple correspondence analysis (MCA) on UK 2003 survey data across music, reading, TV, film, art, sport and food to map a "space of lifestyles." — [Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/Culture-Class-Distinction/Bennett-Savage-Silva-Warde-Gayo-Cal-Wright/p/book/9780203930571); [CRESC working paper](https://hummedia.manchester.ac.uk/institutes/cresc/workingpapers/wp4.pdf)
  - Comparing UK and Australian MCA spaces, class, age and gender were the three most significant axes.
  - Class divisions were more attenuated in Australia.
- **Omnivore thesis.** Peterson & Kern (1996), "Changing Highbrow Taste: From Snob to Omnivore," ASR. — [core.ac.uk PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/305111406.pdf); [Peterson reading](https://is.muni.cz/el/fss/podzim2010/SOC470/SOC_470_Peterson.pdf?lang=cs)
  - Claim: elite status shifted from snobbish exclusion to omnivorous appropriation of many genres.
  - Critiques: the concept is loosely defined; "omnivore" boundaries are not specified; omnivores often still exclude low-status genres.
- **Strong vs. weak readings.** de Vries & Reeves (2020) separate a "strong" reading (omnivores as egalitarians) from a "weak" one; the two predict different taste patterns. — [Olivos / CUHK PDF citing it](https://www.soc.cuhk.edu.hk/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Francisco-Olivos_Sociological-Research-Online.pdf)
- **Contradictory mechanisms.** Goldberg et al. (2016, ASR) note that mechanisms assumed by omnivore proponents yield contradictory predictions about boundary-drawing. Lizardo & Skiles frame omnivorousness as a boundary-drawing mechanism (2013, 2015). — [Goldberg et al. 2016 PDF](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/novelty/2016-goldberg.pdf); [White Rose eprint](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/100789/1/Cultural_Sociology_Post_Review_Pre_edits.docx)
- **Selection vs. influence in a real network.** Lewis, Gonzalez & Kaufman (2012), PNAS 109(1), "Social selection and peer influence in an online social network." About 1,400–1,640 students in one college cohort were followed for 4 years with actor-based models. — [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2012/01/taste-test/); [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2012-01-facebook-patterns-music-movies-friends.pdf)
  - Sharing some music and film tastes predicted becoming friends. **Book taste did not.**
  - Taste rarely spread between friends; the exception was classical/jazz.
- **Why shared music taste bonds people.** Boer, Fischer, Strack, Bond, Lo & Lam (2011), PSPB 37(9), 1159–1171. Two experiments plus a dyadic roommate field study. — [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/why-music-moves-us/201108/music-and-the-ties-that-bind); [VUW summary](https://wgtn.ac.nz/cacr/research/values/sociocultural-differences-in-self-construal-and-subjective-well-being)
  - Music-preference similarity → social attraction, mediated by perceived **value** similarity.
  - Personality similarity was **not** a significant mediator.

### Inferences
- A taste-similarity score likely works as a friendship predictor mostly through music and film, and through what taste signals (values, class or lifestyle, age cohort), not through personality similarity per se.
- Breadth/omnivorousness (number and diversity of genres liked) and a highbrow–lowbrow (cultural capital) axis are well supported in sociology. Both are candidate axes alongside the psychological content factors.

### Gaps
- McPherson, Smith-Lovin & Cook (2001) "Birds of a Feather" (homophily review) and Lonsdale's work on music and social attraction were not retrieved in this session.
- Exact effect sizes for Boer 2011 and Lewis 2012 were not retrieved.

## 6. Cross-domain predictability, and which axes to use vs. myths

### Takeaway
Direct evidence for "music taste predicts film/book taste" is indirect but real: the cross-medium factor structure (Rentfrow 2011) shows that people like the same type of content across media. I found no published out-of-sample accuracy for music → film or music → book prediction. The best-supported axes are the content factors (Communal, Aesthetic/sophisticated, Dark/intense, Cerebral), energy/arousal and valence within music, plus sociological breadth and highbrow–lowbrow.

### Cited Findings
- **Content factors span media.** Shared content factors span music, film, TV and books, with congruence of .95 across samples. Aesthetic, Dark and Communal each load genres from multiple media. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2964424)
- **Personality-based cross-domain recommenders.** Recommender research using Big Five scores to bridge movies, TV, books and music (Facebook data) found personality-based approaches statistically equivalent to or better than rating-based ones in a cross-domain setting. This is a workshop paper, so evidence is weak. — [EMPIRE 2013 paper, CEUR Vol-997](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-997/empire2013_paper_2.pdf)
- **Combining domains helps.** All-domain likes predict openness better (r = .43) than music-only likes (r = .30). This implies non-music domains add non-redundant information. — [Kosinski 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3625324); [Nave 2018](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/personality/conscientiousness/2018-nave.pdf)
- **Domains differ socially.** Books did not predict friendship formation; music and film did. — [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2012/01/taste-test/)

### Inferences
**Best-supported axes for a cross-domain taste space, in rough order of evidence:**
1. Aesthetic / Sophisticated / Depth (Openness-linked, replicated across media and countries)
2. Dark / Intense (Arousal-high, low agreeableness and conscientiousness, younger)
3. Communal / Mellow-Unpretentious (warmth, tenderness, female-skewed)
4. Cerebral / informational (non-music only)
5. Energy/Arousal and Valence (music audio level)
6. Breadth/omnivorousness and highbrow–lowbrow (sociology)

Thrilling is the least reliable.

**Not supported, or myths:**
- That genre taste reveals personality precisely. Single-genre correlations are mostly |r| < .10 (Schäfer & Mehlhorn 2017).
- That music taste reveals neuroticism beyond age and gender (Nave 2018, Study 2).
- That personality similarity is why shared taste bonds people. Values mediated the effect; personality did not (Boer 2011).
- That tastes spread between friends. Selection dominates over influence (Lewis 2012).
- That book taste drives friendship (Lewis 2012).

**Product implications:**
- Compute similarity on latent factor scores rather than raw overlap of items.
- Residualize age and gender, or display them separately.
- Show uncertainty for sparse profiles: accuracy grows with number of items, and spouse-level accuracy needed about 300 likes (Youyou 2015).

### Gaps
- No primary study found reporting r or AUC for predicting film or book preferences from music data alone.
- No validated cross-domain factor model including video games or live events was found.
- Test–retest stability of entertainment factor scores over years is not documented in the retrieved sources.
